# Cornering the Ghost
*Quantum teleportation and the arrival of now*

**Troy Teno | November 2026**
*Essay — Not Peer Reviewed. This is a synthesis and makes no new physics claims. Established marks a published result within its stated assumptions. Reading marks my way of putting established pieces together. Proposed marks a connection that still needs a model or a test. The bold parts are meant to be bold, and the labels say which ones they are.*

## The Question

How does a future become a past, through the now? It is almost too simple a question to ask. Things persist. Time flows. From inside, passage is seamless.

Look closely and the seams appear. Relativity has no single now shared by distant observers. Bell experiments rule out a broad class of local explanations for quantum correlations. The arrow of time comes from entropy and the special state the universe began in. Quantum theory gives probabilities for outcomes, and at the edge where future becomes past, a spread of possibilities becomes one recorded fact. George Ellis calls that edge the growing front of a crystallizing block universe. (Established)

Physics has an equation for how a state changes, and it works. It does not say what it means for the change to become a fact, for a particle in superposition to become a particle here, now. That gap is the measurement problem. It is old, it is open, and this essay does not close it.

What this essay does is narrower. It shows that one exact piece of quantum information theory, teleportation, has the shape of the passage from past to present. It finds the one place where that shape breaks. And it argues that the break is exactly where the mystery has always lived.

## What We Already Know

We know the passage of time occurs. We know the now arrives, and that it arrives steered by the past.

We know the past is overdetermined. It leaves records everywhere, far more than are needed to fix it: memories, photographs, rocks, light still in flight. We know the future is underdetermined. Nothing has recorded it yet. (Established in spirit: Lewis, 1979; Zurek, 2009)

We know a clock reading is a coincidence. Einstein defined the time of an event as the event and the hand of a clock at the same place. No reading of time has ever been made that is not a coincidence of at least two things. (Established: Einstein, 1905)

We know the classical path is completely described. Sum over every possible path, and the paths near the classical one reinforce while the rest cancel. That is why a thrown stone follows the arc it does. (Established)

And we know a quantum state can be carried from one place to another without anyone ever reading it. A single copy of an unknown state cannot be fully read. A measurement gives one outcome, not the whole state.

Put those together and a picture suggests itself. The most precise account we have of carrying a state without reading it is quantum teleportation. If its shape matches the passage of time, the match has to be exact. So the model comes first.

## The Slide

Teleportation was found before it was built. In 1993, Bennett and colleagues noticed that quantum mechanics, exactly as it already stood, allows an unknown state to be transferred without anyone learning what it is. Nobody invented a new law. The protocol was already in the equations. (Established: Bennett et al., 1993)

Start with what is carried. Take a single photon. Its polarization has two possibilities, horizontal and vertical. Each possibility has an amplitude, and the amplitude squared is the probability of finding the photon that way. Between the two possibilities there is also a phase. Phase cannot be seen in either possibility alone. It shows up only by comparison, when the two are brought together. A photon polarized at 45 degrees and a photon polarized in a circle have the same amplitudes, half and half. What separates them is phase, and you can tell them apart only with a filter that compares the two possibilities. The amplitudes plus the phase between them are the state. Nobody knows it. A single measurement of one photon gives one result and destroys the rest. (Established)

Now the entangled pair. A crystal splits one photon into two, sent in opposite directions. Neither photon has a polarization of its own. Measure either one alone and the result is fifty-fifty, every time. What is fixed is the relation between them: their amplitudes are tied together, and so is the phase between the joint possibilities. The pair carries a relation and nothing else. Alice keeps one photon. Bob takes the other. (Established)

Then the measurement. Alice brings the unknown photon and her half of the pair together and compares them. The comparison does not ask what either photon is. It asks only how they relate, and it gives two answers. Are their amplitudes lined up or swapped? Is the phase between them in step, or off by half a turn? Those two answers are two classical bits. They describe a relation, so they say nothing about the unknown state. Alice learns nothing. (Established)

Because Bob's photon was tied to Alice's, her comparison lands on his. Bob's photon now holds the unknown amplitudes and phase, with up to two possible faults: its amplitudes may be swapped, and its phase may be off by half a turn. Alice's two bits say exactly which. Bob swaps back if the first bit says so and shifts the phase back if the second says so. He then holds the original state. Nobody at any step has seen it. (Established)

What travels is the state, not the photon. Bob's photon keeps its own direction and energy. Only its polarization takes on the unknown amplitudes and phase. Alice's photon is absorbed by her detector, so the state exists in one place at a time. Teleportation moves. It never copies. And it does not need anyone to know the state. A known state could simply be described and rebuilt. Only what no one has recorded must be teleported. (Established; the point about records is my reading.)

And herein lies the power. A whole state, every amplitude, every phase, every probability it holds, can be teleported without anyone ever knowing what it represents. Teleportation does not require opacity, but where nature seems to be doing it, opacity is exactly what we find. (Reading)

One of the four results needs no correction. Call it the lucky outcome: Bob's photon already matches, and the state arrives whole. The history here is worth knowing. The first experiment, in Innsbruck in 1997, used a half-silvered mirror to compare the two photons, and with that equipment it could recognize only one of the four results: the lucky one. When it registered, Bob's photon already held the state, with nothing to fix. Full teleportation, with all four results read and corrections applied, came in 2004 with trapped ions. (Established: Bouwmeester et al., 1997; Riebe et al., 2004; Barrett et al., 2004)

*The slide, and why it is possible.* Category theory is a mathematics of connections. It does not ask what things are made of. It asks what they connect to, and how processes chain together. In 2004, Abramsky and Coecke used it to redraw quantum mechanics as pictures. A system is a wire. A process is a box on the wire. An entangled pair is a wire bent into a cup, and Alice's comparison is a wire bent into a cap. Teleportation becomes a single drawing: a cap meeting a cup. Pull the bent wire straight and the state runs from Alice to Bob. The pull is an exact rule of the mathematics. (Established: Abramsky and Coecke, 2004)

The pictures care only about how the wires connect. A box can slide along a wire, and even around a bend, without changing what the drawing means. Nor does the drawing care whether a wire runs across a lab or forward through time. That indifference is what lets the structure slide across the table, from a lab bench to the passage from then to now. Without category theory, the comparison would be a metaphor. With it, the same drawing can be read both ways. (Reading)

*Where the slide started.* I did not begin with time. I began with sentences. Lo, Sadrzadeh and Mansfield measured contextuality in a language model: the same Bell-type statistics physicists use, showing up in how a model reads ambiguous words. That led me to Lambek's grammar and the DisCoCat program, where the cups and caps of teleportation also compose sentences. A word's meaning flows through the grammar the way a state flows through the protocol. Once you have seen the same drawing in physics and in language, you start sliding it across the table everywhere. (Established: Lambek, 1999; Coecke, Sadrzadeh and Clark, 2010; Lo, Sadrzadeh and Mansfield, 2024)

Here is one bold slide, for a later essay. A language model writes one token at a time. Each token goes into the context as a permanent record, an entry in a ledger that cannot be edited, and becomes the input for the next step. Every token arrives already holding the record of the ones before. The output head is a write head, and it has the same shape as the now. (Proposed)

In these pictures, the lucky outcome is the straight wire with nothing on it: a state simply persisting from then to now. It happens one time in four. Every other time, a correction box has to sit on the wire before it runs straight. (Established)

Now make the slide into time. For a reversible law of motion, the law itself can be stored as an entangled pair. That is Choi's result. Teleporting through such a pair carries the state and evolves it on the way, as in gate teleportation. Marletto, Vedral and colleagues showed that ordinary quantum dynamics can be written exactly this way, as a sequence of teleportations in time. They say plainly that it is a formal construction, not something anyone could run. The resource stretches across two times, and no one can hold then and now in hand at once. (Established: Choi, 1975; Gottesman and Chuang, 1999; Marletto, Vedral et al., 2021)

So run the protocol in time. The past is the state being carried. The law is the prepared pair. The measurement is the past touching its environment. The outcome is carried outward as a record, copied into everything nearby until many observers agree. In the lab, Bob would now apply a correction. In time, nobody does. (Reading)

The raw material is already there. The next moment does not need matter shipped in from somewhere else. It is the same system, a moment later. What passes forward is the state. (Reading)

And then the strange part. Bob's state is the next input. Bob becomes Alice, and it happens again. Every now is the receiver of the last step and the sender of the next. You are standing where the message arrives. That is what now is: the arrival of the record. Passage feels seamless from inside because each now arrives already holding the record of the one before. (Reading)

## Where It Breaks, and Why That Is the Point

Check the most important step and it fails. Leave out the bits, and Bob's photon arrives with a random fault. A quarter of the time it has none. A quarter of the time its amplitudes are swapped, a quarter of the time its phase is off by half a turn, and a quarter of the time both. Averaged over all four, nothing survives. A photon sent in horizontal comes out horizontal half the time and vertical half the time. Swapped amplitudes destroy the facts, and shifted phase destroys the interference. That is noise. (Established)

Nature without correction does not deliver noise. It delivers facts. The stone arrives on its arc. So if time is a teleporter, it is not the standard one.

The environment behaves differently, and the reason is physical. Think of light bouncing off a dust grain. The bounced light leaves in a direction that depends on where the grain is, so the light now carries a record of the grain's position. But the light barely pushes the grain. In the ideal case, where the reading involves no push at all, the grain's amplitudes, how likely it is to be here or there, are left exactly as they were. What changes is the phase between "here" and "there." That relation is now shared with light flying off across the room, and it can no longer be compared at the grain. An ideal reading scrambles the phase. It cannot swap the amplitudes. (Established in the ideal case: Zurek, 2003)

A fact is an answer to a question asked in one basis: which slit, which position, which pointer reading. In that basis, the odds of each answer are the amplitudes squared. Phase decides only interference. (Established) So it is reasonable to say the facts live in the amplitudes. A fact is where something is: here, now, no longer in superposition. (Reading)

For the facts, then, the environment's faults never matter. The amplitudes are not swapped. Only the phase is scrambled. Whatever the outcome, the facts arrive untouched, with no key needed. For the facts, every outcome is the lucky one. (Reading, exact within the ideal dephasing model)

Real interactions are not always ideal. A push, an exchange of energy, a decay or a collision can change the amplitudes. A molecule here stays here until something acts on it. Being recorded, on its own, is never enough. (Established)

So the reading is this. The classical world is what a teleporter leaves behind when its only faults are phase faults: the amplitudes come through, and the phase is lost to the surroundings. The environment's faults are phase faults for a physical reason. It records facts by reading them, and reading without pushing leaves amplitudes alone. The whole state is carried. Decoherence sorts what we can read from what we cannot. (Reading)

Notice what changed. In the standard teleporter, survival is existential: there exists one lucky outcome that delivers the state whole. Nature's version changes two things. The question narrows to the facts, the amplitudes, and lets the phase go. And the faults narrow to phase faults, the kind the facts cannot see. With both, survival becomes universal: for every outcome, the facts arrive intact. A one-in-four accident becomes a guarantee. (Reading)

The condition for that flip has a familiar name. Zurek picks out pointer states as the states the environment's interaction leaves undisturbed. That is the same condition stated in different language, which is why the two pictures fit so closely. It also means the fit is a translation, not independent confirmation. (Reading)

That moves the question somewhere precise. Why that basis? Why does the environment read position, and not some other property? Physicists call this the preferred-basis problem, and einselection is the leading answer. Behind it stands a second question that no basis answers: why this outcome, here, now. (Established framework)

The ghost did not vanish when the comparison broke. It moved to a better-known address.

## The Double Slit, Three Ways

A photon passes two slits and lands on a screen. Put it in the same slots. The photon leaving the slits is the carried state. Its flight is the law. Striking the screen is the measurement. The dot is the record, held by the screen and copied by every bit of light that bounces off it, all the way to your eye. There is no correction. You are looking at the dot.

Three words will do a lot of work here, and they are links in one chain. Superposition is the state: both possibilities held together, with their amplitudes and the phase between them. Interference is what a superposition does when its possibilities are brought back together; the phase decides whether they add or cancel. Fringes are the visible pattern interference leaves on a screen. Fringes are possibility left unresolved, made visible. When the fringes disappear, the superposition has not vanished everywhere. Its phase has moved into the surroundings, where it can no longer be compared. (Established)

First, the plain experiment. Nothing records which slit, so the superposition arrives intact. One dot is random and says nothing alone. A thousand dots build the fringes, and the fringes are the proof that the superposition was carried the whole way. The fact is made only at the screen. (Established)

Second, put a which-slit detector at the slits. Now a record of the path exists, and the fringes vanish, whether or not anyone ever reads the detector. Nothing about the law or the screen has changed, only where the record was written. Partial records leave partial fringes, in exactly the trade-off Englert's duality relation bounds. (Established: Englert, 1996)

Third, the quantum eraser. Each photon has a partner carrying the which-slit record. Send the partner through optics that scramble that record into one of two values, and note which comes up. That note is the key. (Established, simplified from Kim et al., 2000)

Look at the screen. It shows a blob. Nothing done to the partners, before or after, changes a single dot. Then sort the dots by their keys. One pile shows fringes. The other shows the same fringes shifted by half a fringe. Put them back together and they cancel into the blob. The fringes were never on the screen. They live in subsets of the data, picked out by the key. Once the dots and keys are written down, the sorting could be done in a spreadsheet a year later. That is a sorting license, not a time machine.

The eraser resembles teleportation with the last step left out. In an ideal modified version, you could detect the partner first, hold the screen photon in a delay line, and shift its phase whenever the key reads the second value. Then the fringes would land on the screen itself. That is the missing step, supplied. Corrections of this kind are routine in teleportation experiments. (Established in principle: Ursin et al., 2004)

So there are three rungs on one ladder. Nothing recorded on the way: the state is carried whole, and the fact is made at the end. Recorded on the way: the fact is made early, and the superposition is spent. Recorded and scrambled: the meaning survives only beside the key. Nature writes the key and never applies it. (Reading)

## The Dial

Here is how the ladder looks from inside time.

A fact does not become firm all at once. The first record of an outcome spends its interference. Every further copy makes the fact easier to recover and harder to dispute. Think of a coffee cup on a table. Light from the cup reaches every eye in the room, and each small piece of that light carries the same answer to "where is the cup?" That is why everyone agrees without comparing notes. Physicists call this quantum Darwinism, and it can be measured. Its limit should be said plainly: redundancy explains why everyone agrees on the outcome. It does not explain why that outcome, and not another. (Established: Zurek, 2009)

Call record redundancy a dial. At one end, nothing is recorded: full superposition, no fact, the open future. At the other, the records are saturated: a hard classical fact, the overdetermined past. Everything we experience as the present sits somewhere in between, facts still hardening as their records spread. The question changes from "did it collapse?" to "how far along the dial is it?" (Proposed)

## The Whole and Its Parts

A correction does not need a person reading a message. A controlled interaction can apply it automatically. But that interaction has to exist. Widening the view does not supply it. (Established: Brassard, Braunstein and Cleve, 1998)

What widening the view does show is that a larger quantum description can hold what none of its parts can reach. Records make selected facts locally stable while the full relation lives only in the whole. (Established)

Teleporting an object made of many parts takes an entangled pair for every part, all held at once. The resource has to be as rich as the thing it carries. In time, the resource would have to be as rich as the world itself, and the world appears to supply it without anyone holding it. (Proposed)

So here is the bold version. Persistence belongs to the whole. Passage belongs to the parts. The whole carries everything forward without loss. A part, inside it, sees records pile up behind it and an open future ahead. The flow of time would be real the way a view is real: it is what the whole looks like from a place within it. (Proposed)

Carry that to the largest scale. The universe as a whole has no outside record and no observer beyond itself. Every fact in it is a relation between one part and another. The whole has nothing outside it to be a fact relative to. (Reading, consistent with Page and Wootters, 1983)

Bell sets the boundary, and a spreadsheet shows it best. In a Bell experiment, Alice picks one of two settings and Bob picks one of two, so the results fill four tabs, one for each combination. Each tab is ordinary data, and where two tabs share a setting, they agree. Bell asked whether one master table could stand behind all four: a single sheet listing what each side would have found at every setting, with each tab just that sheet with some columns hidden. If the world were built from local hidden facts, that master table would exist. Abramsky showed that this is exactly the question Bell's theorem answers. For quantum data, there is no master table. The tabs fit pair by pair and still refuse to glue into one sheet. Nothing in this essay gets around that. (Established: Bell, 1964; Abramsky, 2013)

Everett's relative-state formulation is the nearest existing account. Nothing removes the other outcomes, and each stays correlated with its own record. Where teleportation would apply a correction, Everett's universe applies none, and we live inside the aggregate. The teleportation reading may be Everett's reading in different language. If so, it is still worth showing that a protocol people build in laboratories has the same shape as the oldest no-collapse account of quantum mechanics. (Reading: Everett, 1957)

## What This Does Not Establish

It does not solve the measurement problem. It does not say which outcome occurs, or why. It does not claim that time is literally teleportation, or that nature runs one. The eraser is not a time machine. Choosing a stable basis and explaining why one outcome is experienced are different questions, and this essay reaches only the first. None of it is a new result. It is a way of seeing known results together.

## Cornering the Ghost

In teleportation, nobody ever reads the state. The carried thing alone says nothing. What it carries lives in the relation between the thing and the record of its carrying. Read it too early and you destroy it. Leave out the correction and only the record survives.

Time looks like that from inside. The past is what has been recorded, many times over. The future is what no record yet holds. The now is where the record arrives, already holding the one before, which is why passage feels seamless.

The comparison holds everywhere but one step. A teleporter without correction delivers noise. Nature without correction delivers facts. That step, where nature selects the facts and never corrects, is where the mystery has always lived: which basis, and which outcome, for me, now.

*The protocol is exact. The connection is proposed. The mystery remains.*
